Bar vs No Bar — D1 Flagship

The horizontal crimson rule above the wordmark comes directly from EBB Systems' brand system. This file answers the question: does WizardCD need it?

Font rendering note: The dark variant cards now have -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased applied explicitly. This forces the same rendering mode as macOS uses for light-on-dark text — eliminating the hazy/thinner appearance that made the dark card look different from the light card.
Option 01

With Bar — Current

Recommended: Remove

The bar works on EBB because it's a publishing brand — documents, proposals, letterheads. WizardCD is a product. The bar here says "we borrowed EBB's visual grammar" rather than "we built something new." The crimson CD already handles the accent role.

What the bar does
  • Visually anchors the lockup
  • Direct EBB family signal
  • Editorial authority — Economist / FT feel
What the bar costs
  • Copied verbatim from EBB's design system
  • Redundant — CD accent already provides colour hit
  • Adds complexity in small sidebar (16–19px)
  • Reduces WizardCD's identity as its own product

Option 02

No Bar — Proposed

Recommended: Use This

Remove the bar entirely. The crimson "CD" does all the accent work. The wordmark stands alone — cleaner, more product-like, more distinctly WizardCD. The logo still inherits the EBB family (same typeface, same navy, same crimson) but it's no longer just EBB's formula with a different name.

What removing the bar gives you
  • Stronger, more confident wordmark
  • WizardCD reads as its own product identity
  • Less visual noise in a small sidebar
  • Crimson "CD" carries all the colour weight needed
  • Works better at small sizes (sidebar, favicons, badges)
What you give up
  • Explicit EBB family signalling (intentional)
  • The "editorial rule" rhythm in the lockup

Why the sidebar still works without the bar The crimson in "CD" creates enough visual break at the top of the nav. The nav items sit below a natural boundary (the border-bottom of the logo block). The bar was doing padding work more than design work.
EBB family connection — still there Same typeface (Libre Baskerville), same navy (#0D2240), same crimson (#8B1A1A). Any designer who knows EBB will read the connection immediately. You don't need the bar to signal the relationship.
