WizardCD — Design Directions v2

Ten original directions for WizardCD — each with its own identity, logo treatment, and color logic. The brief: professional, trustworthy, established. Not a startup. Not AI-generated. A tool that has earned its place.

What's different from v1: Each logo is unique to WizardCD — no shared formula. Palettes are drawn from established professional software (AWS Console, HashiCorp, Bloomberg, GitHub) rather than borrowed from EBB. The goal is for WizardCD to have its own confident identity that happens to share EBB's values.
Direction 01

Pure Wordmark

The name is the brand. No icon, no rules, no sub-text — just "WizardCD" in a strong weight. This is how Stripe, Linear, and Vercel present themselves: absolute confidence that the name alone is enough. The CD portion carries the accent color as the only decoration.

Direction 02

Industrial

Deployment is infrastructure work. This direction leans into that — near-black sidebar, amber as the single functional accent color, IBM Plex Sans everywhere. The aesthetic of HashiCorp Vault or Prometheus: tools built for people who keep systems running. The "WIZARD · CD" all-caps logo with a center dot reads as a technical identifier, not a startup name.

Navy Establishment

Direction 05

Zinc & Blue

Clean zinc-gray sidebar — not black, not navy, just a neutral dark that doesn't compete with content. Blue accent for interactivity only. The logo splits the wordmark at the CD: "Wizard" in regular weight, "CD" in bold and accent-colored. This contrast communicates the product name (WizardCD) while emphasizing what it does (CD = continuous deployment). Think GitHub's functional restraint.

Direction 06

Warm Charcoal

Where D2 is cold industrial, this is warm craft. The sidebar is a very dark warm brown-black — the color of aged leather and old printing ink. Parchment content background. Amber-bronze accent. The Lora serif logo gives it the same weight as a century-old engineering firm's letterhead. This is the premium tool direction — the kind you'd pay for without hesitation.

Direction 07

Midnight Indigo

Midnight sidebar (almost but not quite black — it has depth), indigo accent for a modern operations aesthetic. The logo uses a simple square monogram: "W" in a clean box, then "WizardCD" as the wordmark. This is the kind of brand mark that works at 16px favicon size and 200px print size equally. The feel: a NOC dashboard, a serious control plane, built by people who operate at scale.

Direction 08

Blueprint

Ink-blue sidebar and blueprint-white content. This is the aesthetic of a technical document — precise, considered, designed for people who read spec sheets. The logo uses a solid filled circle in sky-blue followed by IBM Plex Sans — clean and technical like a Cloudflare or Tailscale product. Every color choice has functional intent: blue sidebar = infrastructure layer, white content = work surface.

Direction 09

Light Sidebar

Every other direction uses a dark sidebar — this one inverts that. A warm stone/cream sidebar with the content area slightly off-white. The navy is the accent, used for active states and data. This reads like a premium editorial product — the Financial Times, the Economist's data tools, Reuters Eikon. The Playfair Display logo, sitting on a light background, has a completely different presence: calm, authoritative, permanent.

Direction 10

Condensed Authority

The most minimal of all — deep blue-gray sidebar, white content, a single strong red used ONCE (active nav item left border only). The logo is a confident abbreviation: "W·CD" — the shorthand a team actually uses when talking about their deployment tool. No expanded name in the sidebar. The philosophy: if you need to read the full name to know what this tool is, the design has already failed. Your team will call it "W·CD" within a week anyway.
